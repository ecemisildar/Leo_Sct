PIPELINE:

Multi-robot control often requires expert knowledge to ensure safe and reliable behavior. Formal methods can provide these guarantees, but designing formal specifications is time-consuming and typically requires domain expertise. We present a method that allows non-expert users to describe robot tasks in natural language and automatically obtain safe robot behaviors.

Our approach uses Large Language Models to translate user instructions into formal specifications for Supervisory Control Theory. We evaluate it in a multi-robot coverage task, where ground rovers explore an unknown environment while avoiding obstacles and each other.

To study the effect of task description, we compare controllers generated from four prompts with increasing levels of detail. Each prompt is tested in five runs using two large language models, resulting in 40 generated controllers. We compare their performance in terms of coverage and collisions.

Results show that large language models can generate valid robot controllers for this task. In simulation, the best controllers achieve up to 65% coverage with only 7 collisions, while GPT-5.4 produces more reliable controllers overall than GPT-4.1.


ROBOT EXPERIMENT:

In this real-world experiment, three Leo Rovers execute the best-performing controller generated by GPT-5.4 and synthesized using Supervisory Control Theory. The robots operate for three minutes in a 3 by 2 meters bounded environment while an overhead camera records the experiment for offline evaluation of coverage and collision counts.

Each robot selects actions such as moving forward, moving backward, random walk, or rotating clockwise, counterclockwise, or fully in place. These actions are chosen based on feedback from the onboard RGB-D cameras, which detect obstacles on the left, right, or directly in front of the robot. Using these local observations, each robot independently applies its synthesized supervisor to continuously select safe actions, allowing the team to keep exploring while avoiding collisions.
